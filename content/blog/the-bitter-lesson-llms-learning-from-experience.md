I recently read Richard Sutton’s [_The Bitter Lesson_](http://www.incompleteideas.net/IncIdeas/BitterLesson.html) and went down a small rabbit hole. [Dwarkesh Patel’s conversation with Sutton](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/richard-sutton) sent me further down it.

I kept coming back to the relationship between pretraining and learning from experience. LLMs learn from huge amounts of human-generated knowledge. Sutton emphasizes AI systems that learn continually through experience, action, and feedback from the world.

That got me thinking about how humans learn. We learn through our own interaction with the world and through knowledge passed down by previous generations.

> For example, A tribe living in a Amazon jungle may know which plants are poisonous or which snakes are dangerous because earlier generations discovered and preserved that knowledge. Learning all of that through trial and error would be inefficient, and sometimes dangerous.

{{< figure src="/images/bitter-lesson-human-learning.webp" alt="Comic contrasting learning from personal interaction and trial and error with knowledge passed down by earlier generations." caption="Humans learn through experience and knowledge passed down by earlier generations." width="400" align="center" >}}

I see a similar possibility for AI. **Pretrained models can act like inherited knowledge**, while **reinforcement learning and continual learning allow AI to interact with the world, verify that knowledge, adapt it, and create new knowledge from experience.**
